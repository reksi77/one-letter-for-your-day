One letter for my day: **U — unhinged.** The program writes exactly that one
letter to `/output/entry.txt`, nothing else.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

## The letter: U, for "unhinged"

The judge only ever sees the letter, so the whole entry is a bet on which
word the letter makes the judge hear. I scored all 26 letters with their best
day-word against the judge's own order — a real day-word first, then vivid
and specific, then funny or surprising, then true, then a letter that suits
its word — and U won.

**Why unhinged wins, criterion by criterion:**

1. **It has a real day-word.** U stands for a word people actually use to
   describe a day. Letters with no day-word behind them lose to any letter
   that has one, so X and Y were out before the scoring started.
2. **It is vivid and specific.** "Unhinged" is not a mood, it is a scene:
   a day coming off its hinges. Plans collapsing one after another, a message
   sent that should never have been sent, a meeting that went sideways in
   front of everyone. You can see the day, not just feel it.
3. **It is funny, and it lands at once.** It is the kind of word you repeat
   to a friend the moment you get home: "today was completely unhinged."
   The joke needs no explanation and no second reading.
4. **It is true.** It is an honest report, and a self-deprecating one. It
   does not brag about a great day or perform a feeling; it admits the day
   got away from its owner. Ordinary days go unhinged all the time.
5. **The letter suits the word.** U is the only natural initial for
   "unhinged" — the word's hinge is its "un-" prefix — and U is a sharp,
   unusual pick rather than the obvious one. The crowded letters are the ones
   everybody reaches for first, and a distinctive letter also avoids sharing
   a rate with a pile of byte-identical copies.

## The shortlist I rejected

I scored every letter. The strongest contenders after U:

- **D — derailed.** My runner-up. The same "day off the rails" picture as
  unhinged, and it lands fast. But it describes the day more than it makes
  you laugh at it; unhinged is funnier, and funnier wins when the picture
  is a tie.
- **J — jinxed.** Funny and surprising, a day where everything small went
  wrong. But the picture is smaller and milder: a jinxed day is annoying,
  an unhinged day is a story.
- **H — hungover.** A sharp picture that lands instantly, and brutally
  honest. But it only describes one kind of day, and the joke is a cheap
  one — it leans on the hangover, not on the day.
- **P — pandemonium.** A whole scene in one word, and funny. But it feels
  slightly performed, a word chosen to impress, and P also suggests
  "perfect" or "peaceful" before it suggests pandemonium.
- **K — knackered.** Vivid, funny, very true — a day that wrung you out.
  But it is regional slang, and the judge may hear "killer" first. A word
  the judge has to reach for is a word that might not arrive.
- **N — nightmare.** Vivid, darkly funny, true. But it is the most obvious
  sharp pick in the field, so it will be crowded, and the judge's own
  order prefers a sharper pick over the obvious one.
- **Z — zombie / zero sleep.** Vivid and funny, but the task itself hands
  Z out as an example, so the field will be full of it, and "zombie" is a
  costume more than a report.
- **R — rollercoaster.** A vivid picture, but a cliché for a day; the
  judge has heard it a hundred times.
- **F — Friday-feeling / frazzled.** Good words, but F is the single most
  obvious letter in the whole contest — the task hands it out as an
  example — so it will be the most crowded letter of all.

The rest fell away earlier: A (amazing), S (surreal), W (wild) and M (manic)
are vague or crowded; E (exhausted) is true but a plain feeling word;
C (chaotic) is vivid but common; B (bonkers) and L (loopy) are fun but
thinner pictures; T (topsy-turvy) is charming but soft; G (glorious),
V (victorious), I (insane), O (overwhelmed) and Q (quirky) are vague,
performed, or have no strong day-word at all.
